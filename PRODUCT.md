# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Backend and platform engineers who own long-running coding agents and need to decide whether
the whole application remains correct after Temporal recovery. They use this surface while
designing failure handling, reviewing an architecture, or investigating a concrete recovery
boundary. Their primary task is to compare an unsafe control with the smallest protected
mechanism, then inspect the exact evidence behind the verdict.

## Product Purpose

Make the Agent Durability Lab's admitted evidence usable without weakening it. A user should
reach one trustworthy recovery quickly, understand stable identity, authority, effect, and
cancellation boundaries, replay the native history, and attribute each guarantee to Temporal,
the application, or the destination. Presentation succeeds only when it remains downstream of
the independent oracle and makes claim limits and falsifiers easier to see.

## Brand Personality

Exact, calm, candid. The product should feel like a well-run incident review: technically
serious, plain about failures, and confident because every conclusion is inspectable.

## Anti-references

- Generic agent cookbook galleries that optimize for breadth and copy-paste snippets.
- Polished Workflow demos that imply recovery correctness without a negative control.
- Neon observability dashboards, glass panels, decorative terminal motifs, and metric-card grids.
- Interfaces that hide provenance, correction lineage, or raw failure evidence behind a verdict.
- Claims of exactly-once effects, provider compatibility, or performance without the required evidence.

## Design Principles

1. Show the failure before the mechanism. Unsafe and protected outcomes stay comparable.
2. Keep evidence adjacent to every conclusion. Native history and raw artifacts remain one action away.
3. Separate procedure, authority, and effect. Responsibility is visible, not buried in prose.
4. Use progressive disclosure. Start with one recovery story, then reveal identity and provenance detail.
5. Treat presentation as non-authoritative. It renders verified records and never chooses a verdict.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Every workflow must be keyboard operable, status must never rely on color
alone, focus must remain visible, semantic landmarks and headings must be correct, and motion
must respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Dense evidence views must reflow on narrow screens and
retain readable labels for assistive technology.
